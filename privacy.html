Privacy Policy

This Privacy Policy applies to the GeoTime application by Matthew F. Carroll, currently available as a web app, on iOS and on Android.

GeoTime collects nothing about you by default, and no account is needed to use it. The app requests access to your device's location for the sole purpose of displaying the current time and timezone at your location. Your coordinates are used only while the app is running, and are processed entirely locally on your device. Your location is never transmitted to our servers or to any third party. The app stores the name of the city or town closest to your last known position on your device, so the home-screen widget can label your local time while the app is closed.

One optional feature does send something, and only if you turn it on. It is described in “Sharing your time” below.

GeoTime can also show “ship time” — the onboard clock on a Royal Caribbean or Celebrity cruise, which shifts during a sailing. Because that clock is set by the crew and cannot be worked out from a map, the app fetches the current ship time from Royal Caribbean when you add a ship to your world clock list, and while you are connected to a ship's wi-fi, to recognise which ship you are aboard. Those requests identify only the ship name — never your location, device or account identifiers, or personal information of any kind.

Sharing your time

GeoTime can show you what time it is for someone else — a partner in another country, a parent on a cruise — on your world clock list and your home-screen widget. This is entirely optional. Nothing described in this section happens, and no record of you exists on our server, unless and until you tap “Share your time” or enter somebody's code.

When you do, our server creates an account for you under a random identifier, and gives this device a random key of its own. The server stores only a scrambled form of that key, so its records cannot be used to act as you. None of it is tied to your email address, phone number, your device's own hardware identifiers, or any account you hold elsewhere. There is no password, because there is nothing to sign in to.

The sharing feature asks you for a name. That is sent to our servers and the people you choose to share your time with see it above your clock, so they know whose time it is. Put whatever you like in there, it is just a label for them to recognize you. Whoever you share with can rename you on their own device afterwards, and that renaming happens entirely on their phone.

While sharing is on, the app sends the server what time it is where you are, and nothing more. In practice that is a timezone name such as America/Vancouver — or, aboard a ship, that ship's name and how many hours its clock is from UTC.

The exact timezone label is only shared if you choose. By default, the only thing our server passes on is how many hours your clock is from theirs — a number, with no region attached. Turning on “Share my exact timezone” tells them the timezone by name, for example "Vancouer" or "Los Angeles". The sharing screen shows you a preview of what they will see. Either way they see what time it is for you and not where you are.

No history is kept. When you change timezones, each update replaces the last, so there is no record of where you have been or when. We store when it last updated, to display when a clock has gone stale. We keep a record of when your account and each linked device were created and last used, and when each share was made and accepted — dates only, never places — that lets you tell your devices apart and enables us to eventually remove an account nobody has opened in a long time.

The name you give somebody you follow — “Mum”, “Dad” — is stored only on your own device and is never sent anywhere.

You can use the same account on more than one device — a tablet, or a browser on your computer — by linking it. Your existing device has to approve the new one before it can see anything. The time you share with others comes from your main device. For each device linked to your account, the server records what kind it is (“iPhone”, “Chrome on macOS”), whether it is your main device, and when it was last used, so that you can recognise each one and remove it under “Your devices”.

Sharing codes are single-use and stop working after 24 hours. A share can be removed at any time: removing somebody from your list stops their time reaching you, and removing a follower stops your time reaching them. Accepting a share does not allow that person to see your time. You have to explicitly choose to also share your time with them, if you both want to see each other's clocks.

The server is a small application run by us on Cloudflare's network, which processes the requests on our behalf. Nothing is sold, shared with advertisers, or used to build a profile, and there is no analytics or tracking in the app at all.

Deleting your data

In GeoTime, open the Sharing card and tap Delete my sharing data at the bottom, then confirm. That deletes your account, every device linked to it, your name, your current time, and every share you are part of, immediately and permanently. You will stop seeing the people you follow, and the people who follow you stop seeing your time. Nothing is retained after deletion, and there is no backup copy to ask us for.

You can do this from any of your linked devices, including a browser linked at geotime.app. To remove just one device and keep the rest, tap Remove or Unlink beside it under “Your devices” instead.

If you no longer have any device with GeoTime on it, nothing we hold can be matched to you: there is no email address, phone number or password on the account, so we have no way to tell which one was yours. If you have never tapped “Share your time” or followed anybody, there is nothing on our server to delete.
